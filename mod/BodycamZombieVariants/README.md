# Bodycam Zombie Variants — 0.1.0 candidate

A separate host extension for the available native game modes. Original objectives, player-kill scoring, maps and round rules remain native. Host cards and the real lobby `Gamemode` attribute use `Z-` names. Ordinary Deathmatch becomes `Z-Deathmatch (Classic)`. The existing `Z-Deathmatch` keeps its gameplay; its requested round audio is prepared in this separate extension and remains muted pending the correct cue.

## Behavior

- Owned zombies take 100% of normal body damage and normal head damage. Verified stock paths cover firearms and knives; grenade radial hits retain native distance falloff. The mod damage factor is 1.0 throughout; native weapon, head-bonus and distance calculations remain intact. Body hits still trigger the existing sprint response.
- Owned zombie kills do not increment native kill/death counters or XP. In native score-limit modes (Deathmatch and Team Deathmatch), every five credited zombie kills award one normal match point: per player in Deathmatch, pooled per team in Team Deathmatch. The bonus respects the native score limit and round completion. Fractions reset between rounds and worlds; duplicate death callbacks and cleanup do not count. Modes without the native score-limit rule receive no zombie bonus, preserving their objective rules. Player kills pass through normally.
- Human players and the game's verified AI_Humanoid match bots participate in death observation and zombie targeting. Zombie pawns never enter this roster.
- Revenge zombies use the actual death position and original killer identity. With a valid killer, they immediately run at speed 450; successful exact-target assignment is required before the spawn is counted and the player-corpse callback is committed. Death-location spawns have a separate one-second attempt interval, within the living/revenge caps. A temporarily refused spawn is retried within the original bounded ten-second death queue; missing or lost targets produce roaming behavior. A full cap can still prevent a spawn before its deadline.
- After a revenge zombie is successfully created and validated, an optional adapter hides only the exact dead player/bot pawn captured for that death and disables its host collision. Failed, deferred or expired spawn requests leave the corpse alone. Native death/respawn callbacks keep running; destruction is deferred until the observed native finish callback has returned and the pawn is safely detached, otherwise normal native lifespan cleanup remains responsible. Reuse of the same pawn is guarded by exact life identity; a confirmed revived pawn restores this adapter's visibility/collision changes from its saved originals. The hidden flag uses the native replicated actor property; guest rendering and collision still require a network playtest. Zombie corpses retain their separate 25-second behavior.
- Ambient zombies wander and detect nearby players. Their replenishment follows a smooth 45-second wave with a positive trough, adapting to confirmed zombie deaths in the last 30 seconds. Attempts are spaced 1–10 seconds apart; below two ambient zombies the interval is at most three seconds, subject to spawn safety and capacity. Waves change replenishment, not existing zombies or round rules. Body hits make a zombie run at the actual attacker at speed 630, 40% above the ordinary run speed 450. Normal chasing (140) and wandering (90) retain their previous speeds; repeated hits do not compound the increase. Target changes stop the old native attack before switching.
- Other eligible living zombies occasionally run at speed 450 for four seconds, with individually staggered 20–40-second pauses and at most one autonomous start per update. They pursue a valid existing or perceived target, or use native patrol without inventing a target. Body-hit pursuit at 630 takes priority over these bursts. Explicit sprint starts use the existing optional vocal cue and audio budget.
- Remaining within 2 m for 20 seconds can spawn up to two camper zombies. Rearm requires moving 6 m and a 90-second cooldown.
- Despawn requires both distance beyond 120 m from every living player and 90 seconds without interaction. Active fights refresh interaction time.
- At the native end of a round or match, the next existing 500 ms update removes all zombies owned by this extension, including retained zombie corpses. Spawns are restricted to the native active-round phase; waiting deaths and per-round replenishment state are discarded before a new round starts. A changed native round identity also forces cleanup if the end phase occurred between updates. Failed removals remain tracked and block the next round's spawns until cleanup is confirmed. Weather remains held through intermission, and the existing player-corpse adapter keeps its deferred native cleanup.
- Dead zombies stop receiving AI commands and remain for up to 25 seconds for native ragdoll playback. The shipped death path already stops the brain and actor tick; this extension adds no corpse simulation. After native death is confirmed for an exact owned actor, routine validation retains a fresh actor-liveness check but skips redundant death/controller queries. Early native removal and world cleanup remain guarded. At most eight corpses are retained separately from the fourteen living zombies; cleanup failures reserve their slots until removal is confirmed.
- Each supported outdoor world holds 18:00 across prematch and match. The native sky's default dusk time is 18:00; actual brightness still depends on the map's sky settings. Where the native weather system exists, persistent rain, full cloud cover and fog are applied and restored on opt-out. Cloud Coverage remains 10, Rain 7.5 and Fog 9. Host-local fog color intensity is 50% of the saved original. The color and smoothing inputs are host-local; weather controls use the native replication path. Indoor maps without a supported sky retain their native lighting and can still spawn zombies. Missing weather or cosmetic tint never blocks spawning; an actual sky-clock update failure remains a readiness failure.
- Where supported, the optional host-local `Sky Light Intensity` input is held at 110% of its saved original for a slight increase in diffuse ambient light. Native time, cloud, interior and other light factors remain in control. The original input is restored on opt-out, probes are bounded and stable updates do not rebuild or recapture the sky. An unsupported cosmetic light input does not block zombies. This is a 10% input-intensity increase, not a guaranteed 10% pixel-brightness increase or identical guest rendering.
- Optional original zombie vocalizations accompany target acquisition, sprint onset and attacks, with staggered occasional calls between actions. The shipped spatial multicast attaches finite cues to the zombie's head; looping cues are never restarted. Per-zombie cooldowns and a global dispatch limit prevent stacked calls. Audio failure cannot disable gameplay or host cards. The host confirmed audible voices on CQB; guest playback remains unverified.
- Standard height fog remains active. The previously verified clear-weather start-distance multiplier of about 0.56616 is retained. The native density scale and its start-distance zero-density threshold are both multiplied by 0.35, reducing the previous paired0.5 density by30% while preserving the start ratio and approximate10m onset. UDS keeps control of its interior curve and cache updates. This is a host-local approximation, not an exact linear 10–80 m fade; 80 m remains the visual target. Volumetric fog is not enabled by this change, and cutoff, cloud cover, rain, raw Fog control and time remain unchanged. Stable ticks do not rebuild the sky; saved inputs are restored on opt-out. The user accepted this thinner, darker atmosphere on CQB as nearly perfect; only general illumination needed a slight increase.
- Round-start and round-end audio is prepared for the new variants and existing Z-Deathmatch. Native client sound calls include guests and spectators; repeated phase notifications do not replay a boundary. Playback stays muted until the exact bells/choir cue from Cemetery is identified and confirmed. Muted playback never loads or requires the candidate sound, and audio availability cannot disable gameplay or host cards. GameCouldownMusic, EventMusic and RoundStart were rejected in listening checks.

Defaults: 14 living zombies plus at most 8 retained corpses; ambient/revenge/camper caps 6/6/2; ambient target 6. Camper attempts retain their five-second interval and death-location attempts one second. All sources share a maximum of one attempt per 500 ms tick; ambient attempts are at least one second apart. After four death attempts, due ambient work gets priority. The wave multiplier is 0.5 + ((1 - cos(2*pi*elapsed/45))/2)^2, ranging from 0.5 to 1.5. Adaptive history, phase and deadlines reset at native round boundaries, when leaving a variant or changing worlds; missed deadlines never accumulate spawn credits. Settings are in `Scripts/variant_ai.lua`. Change files only while the game is closed.

## Install and run

Close Bodycam completely. Extract the candidate package and run `Install.ps1` in PowerShell. Use `Install.ps1 -UpdateExisting` to update this separate mod; the previous directory is retained as a backup outside Mods. It verifies package hashes and supported game/UE4SS versions, refuses a running game, and installs only `BodycamZombieVariants`. It never replaces Z-Deathmatch files.

Start Bodycam normally and select a new `Z-` host card. The native companion loads once; live reload is disabled. Unsupported binaries or unavailable native hooks keep variant cards disabled. This build targets the inspected Steam build 25505592 and the exact executable/UE4SS hashes in the package manifest.

The legacy F10 control remains unchanged. If it activates the old Z-Deathmatch in the same world, this extension detects its status at the next 500 ms update and cleans up its own actors. Do not use F10 to start a new variant.

## Acceptance status

Native interception, Lua behavior, lifecycle cleanup and six real x64 detours passed isolated checks. Earlier cold playtests confirmed visible zombies on Z-Deathmatch (Classic)/Paintball, indoor spawning after prematch on The Backrooms, and visible zombies on Z-Team Deathmatch/CQB. Lobby names, 0.1 body scaling, preserved head damage, native AI binding, target changes and roaming were recorded.

The owner-corrected native bridge passed activation on CQB: the extension became ACTIVE, recognized one human and nine native match bots, spawned revenge zombies at recorded bot death positions, retained a dead zombie for about 25 seconds and recorded four native match points from 24 qualifying kills, with four remaining fifths across the teams. The user confirmed visible zombies, death-location spawns and retained corpses, but reported severe periodic frame drops. Visible kill/XP counters under the new bonus rule, final score-limit completion, actual health deltas and guest behavior remain pending.

The 1695-check performance build was cold-tested on Z-Team Deathmatch/Paintball. The user confirmed smooth gameplay and visible fog. Telemetry verified rain/fog application, native targeting, damage interception and match-point awards. That build removed redundant one-second sky rebuilds, repeated player inventories in hit/death callbacks, unrelated Blueprint reward work and excessive diagnostic writes. Gameplay still updates every 500 ms; detailed status is written every two seconds or on a state change. `os.clock` samples measure script work, not GPU frame times.

The same playtest found many death requests expiring without an attempt (68 of 108 at one snapshot) behind the shared five-second spawn interval. It also found the second map, CQB, inactive with a missing optional audio object recorded. Review found two independent ways to lose activation: a readiness gap could skip the sole mode publication, and a transition map was treated as a return to the lobby. The 1723-check candidate separated death-spawn cadence, removed audio as a gameplay prerequisite, preserved confirmed mode publication through temporary readiness gaps and retained selection through transition maps.
